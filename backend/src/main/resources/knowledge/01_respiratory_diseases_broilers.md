# Veterinary Compendium: Respiratory Diseases in Commercial Broilers

## 1. Newcastle Disease (ND / Avian Paramyxovirus-1)
- **Etiology:** Virulent Avian Orthoavulavirus 1 (NDV). Highly contagious viral disease affecting domestic poultry.
- **Key Clinical Symptoms:**
  - Acute onset of high mortality (often 50% to 100% in unvaccinated flocks).
  - Respiratory distress: gasping, coughing, sneezing, rattling tracheal sounds (rales).
  - Neurological signs: torticollis (twisted neck), circling, tremors, complete leg/wing paralysis.
  - Digestive signs: profuse greenish or bright yellowish watery diarrhea.
  - Facial edema: swelling around eyes and head; dark or cyanotic comb and wattles.
- **Differential Diagnosis:** Avian Influenza (AI), Infectious Laryngotracheitis (ILT), Infectious Bronchitis (IB).
- **Critical Action & Vet Warning:**
  - Newcastle Disease is a notifiable viral infection with NO direct cure. Antibiotics have zero effect on the ND virus.
  - Suspected outbreaks require IMMEDIATE quarantine of the shed and immediate notification of a licensed poultry veterinarian and local animal health authorities.
  - Strict biosecurity: disinfect footbaths, restrict farm visitors, incinerate or deeply bury dead carcasses.

## 2. Infectious Bronchitis (IB)
- **Etiology:** Avian Coronavirus causing acute upper respiratory tract infection.
- **Key Clinical Symptoms:**
  - Severe gasping, coughing, snicking, and wet tracheal rales, especially noticeable at night.
  - Watery eyes (lacrimation), swollen sinuses, and nasal exudate.
  - Sharp drop in feed consumption and sudden growth arrest.
  - Significant predisposition to secondary bacterial infections (mainly *Escherichia coli* airsacculitis and pericarditis).
- **Management & Treatment Protocol:**
  - Provide supportive therapy: mucolytics/expectorants (bromhexine, menthol-eucalyptus essential oil sprays).
  - Elevate shed temperature by 1°C to 2°C to reduce chilling stress.
  - Prevent secondary bacterial colibacillosis with veterinarian-prescribed water medication if mortality begins climbing.

## 3. Chronic Respiratory Disease (CRD / Mycoplasma gallisepticum)
- **Etiology:** *Mycoplasma gallisepticum* (MG), frequently complicated by *E. coli* (*Complex CRD* / CCRD).
- **Precipitating Factors:** High ammonia levels (> 20 ppm), poor ventilation, dust, cold stress, or recent live viral vaccination reactions.
- **Key Clinical Symptoms:**
  - Persistent snicking (sneezing clicks), foamy eye discharge, nasal discharge, swollen infraorbital sinuses.
  - Decreased daily feed intake, slow weight gain, elevated Feed Conversion Ratio (FCR).
- **Veterinary Protocol:**
  - Improve shed ventilation immediately to clear ammonia below 15 ppm.
  - Under veterinary supervision, targeted macrolides (e.g., Tylosin, Tilmicosin) or tetracyclines (Doxycycline) administered via drinking water according to strict product label and withdrawal rules.

## 4. Infectious Coryza
- **Etiology:** *Avibacterium paragallinarum* (Gram-negative bacterium).
- **Key Clinical Symptoms:**
  - Acute swelling of the face, eyelids, and wattles (puffy face / owl head appearance).
  - Thick, foul-smelling (fetid) nasal discharge and sticky eyelid closure.
  - Rapid drop in feed and water intake within 24 to 48 hours.
- **Veterinary Protocol:**
  - Clean drinking water lines and flush with chlorine/sanitizer.
  - Consult a veterinarian for sensitive antimicrobial treatment (e.g., sulfonamides or fluoroquinolones with strict withdrawal monitoring).

## 5. Aspergillosis (Brooder Pneumonia)
- **Etiology:** *Aspergillus fumigatus* (fungal infection from moldy litter, damp wood shavings, or contaminated hatchery eggs).
- **Key Clinical Symptoms:**
  - Fast, heavy breathing and gasping with NO rattling sound (silent gasping) in 1 to 14-day-old chicks.
  - High early chick mortality; affected chicks are depressed and unthrifty.
  - *Antibiotics do not work and will exacerbate fungal overgrowth.*
- **Action:**
  - Immediately remove moldy bedding or caked damp litter. Disinfect drinker surroundings and improve dry ventilation.
