# Veterinary Compendium: Immunosuppressive, Viral & Critical Pathologies

## 1. Infectious Bursal Disease (IBD / Gumboro Disease)
- **Etiology:** Birnavirus targeting the Bursa of Fabricius (the primary B-lymphocyte immune organ of young chickens).
- **Vulnerable Age:** 2 to 6 weeks of age (Days 14 to 35).
- **Key Clinical Symptoms:**
  - Severe flock depression, reluctance to move, tremors, ruffled feathers.
  - White watery or chalky diarrhea, severe dehydration with darkened skin.
  - Vent picking / pecking behavior due to inflammation of the cloacal bursa.
  - Spiking mortality curve starting on day 3 post-infection, peaking rapidly on days 4–5, and dropping by day 7.
- **Immunosuppressive Impact:** Surviving birds develop severe, permanent immune suppression, failing to respond to subsequent vaccines and succumbing to secondary *E. coli* or fungal infections.
- **Management:**
  - No specific viral cure. Provide intensive supportive electrolyte and vitamin therapy in drinking water to mitigate kidney dehydration lesions.
  - Future prevention: Strictly optimize hatchery and on-farm Gumboro vaccination timing based on maternal antibody titers.

## 2. Marek's Disease
- **Etiology:** Alphaherpesvirus causing lymphoproliferative tumors and peripheral nerve degeneration.
- **Key Symptoms:**
  - Progressive asymmetric paralysis: classic posture is one leg stretched forward and the other stretched backward.
  - Drooping wings, twisted neck, enlarged feather follicles, and gray/irregular eye pupils.
- **Prevention:**
  - Vaccination at Day 0 in the hatchery (*in-ovo* at Day 18 of incubation or subcutaneous injection upon hatch) is mandatory. There is no treatment for affected clinical birds.

## 3. Highly Pathogenic Avian Influenza (HPAI)
- **Etiology:** Orthomyxovirus (Subtypes H5 and H7).
- **Key Clinical Symptoms:**
  - Catastrophic sudden mass mortality (flocks dying within 24 to 48 hours with minimal prior symptoms).
  - Cyanosis (dark purple/blue discoloration) and severe swelling of combs, wattles, and head.
  - Subcutaneous petechial hemorrhages on the unfeathered shanks and hocks.
  - Blood-tinged nasal discharge and extreme respiratory gasping.
- **MANDATORY EMERGENCY PROTOCOL:**
  - Avian Influenza is a severe zoonotic and international notifiable threat.
  - **NEVER attempt home treatment.** Immediately isolate the premises, lock down all vehicle and human movement, and report to governmental veterinary and public health departments immediately.
