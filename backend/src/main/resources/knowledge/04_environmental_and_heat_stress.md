# Veterinary Compendium: Environmental Management & Heat Stress Alleviation

## 1. Broiler Thermal Comfort & Heat Stress Physiology
- Broiler chickens lack sweat glands and rely entirely on panting (evaporative cooling from the respiratory tract) and sensible heat loss (radiation, convection via unfeathered body surfaces).
- **Critical Threshold:** When ambient shed temperature exceeds 30°C (86°F), or when the Temperature-Humidity Index (THI) is elevated (Temperature in °F + Relative Humidity % > 160), birds experience severe thermal distress.
- Birds older than 21 days with heavy breast muscle and dense plumage are at extreme risk of acute heat prostration and suffocation.

## 2. Clinical Signs of Heat Stress
- Rapid open-mouth panting with extended neck.
- Spreading wings away from the body to expose bare skin.
- Complete lethargy, lying flat on the litter (prostration).
- Water consumption increases by 2.0x to 3.0x normal rates, while feed intake plummets.
- Rapid mortality spikes during peak afternoon heat (1:00 PM – 5:00 PM).

## 3. Practical Heat Stress Management Actions
1. **Tunnel Ventilation & Air Velocity:**
   - Maintain minimum air speed of **2.5 to 3.0 meters per second** directly over the birds to generate wind-chill cooling.
2. **Water Line Temperature Management:**
   - Birds will refuse drinking water if temperature exceeds 25°C (77°F).
   - Flush water lines 2 to 3 times daily with cool fresh well water.
3. **Evaporative Cooling Pads:**
   - Run cooling pads in short, intermittent cycles.
   - *CRITICAL WARNING:* Turn off cooling pads or foggers if shed relative humidity exceeds 75–80%, as high humidity disables evaporative panting and kills birds faster.
4. **Shift Feeding Times:**
   - Withdraw feed 2 to 3 hours before peak afternoon heat. Feed birds during cooler early morning (5:00 AM – 8:00 AM) and night hours (8:00 PM – midnight) to avoid dietary metabolic heat during peak ambient temperatures.
5. **Electrolyte & Antioxidant Supplementation:**
   - Supplement drinking water with **Sodium Bicarbonate, Potassium Chloride, and Vitamin C (Ascorbic Acid at 1g/L)** to combat respiratory alkalosis caused by hyperventilation.

## 4. Brooding Temperature Guidelines for Young Chicks
- **Day 1 to 3:** 32°C – 34°C (90°F – 93°F), RH 60%–70%
- **Day 4 to 7:** 30°C – 32°C (86°F – 90°F)
- **Day 8 to 14:** 28°C – 30°C (82°F – 86°F)
- **Day 15 to 21:** 26°C – 28°C (78°F – 82°F)
- **Day 22+:** 20°C – 22°C (68°F – 72°F)
- **Air Quality Standards:** Carbon Dioxide < 3,000 ppm, Ammonia < 15 ppm, Dust < 3.4 mg/m³.
