# Veterinary Compendium: Enteric & Digestive Disorders in Broilers

## 1. Coccidiosis (*Eimeria* spp.)
- **Etiology:** Protozoan parasite of the genus *Eimeria* (*E. tenella* causes cecal coccidiosis; *E. maxima*, *E. acervulina*, and *E. necatrix* cause intestinal coccidiosis).
- **Peak Occurrence:** Day 14 to Day 32 in broiler flocks, especially in humid conditions or wet litter areas (around nipple drinkers).
- **Key Clinical Symptoms:**
  - Bright red blood or dark brownish mucus in droppings / feces.
  - Pale comb, beak, and shanks (anemia from intestinal hemorrhage).
  - Severe lethargy, ruffled feathers, huddling together with eyes closed.
  - Rapid drop in feed consumption and spike in daily mortality.
- **Immediate Veterinary Action Protocol:**
  - Start anticoccidial water treatment immediately under veterinary guidance (e.g., Amprolium, Toltrazuril, or Sulfaclozine).
  - Administer Vitamin K and Vitamin A supplementation in water to accelerate blood clotting and mucosal tissue repair.
  - Top-dress or remove wet, caked litter immediately to break the oocyst sporulation cycle.

## 2. Necrotic Enteritis (NE)
- **Etiology:** *Clostridium perfringens* (toxin-producing spore-forming anaerobic bacterium), almost always secondary to mucosal damage from subclinical coccidiosis or dietary high-viscosity grains.
- **Key Clinical Symptoms:**
  - Dark, foul-smelling, wet diarrhea containing sloughed mucosal debris ("Turkish towel" gut lining).
  - Severe depression, sudden death in healthy, well-fleshed birds within hours.
  - Sharp upward spike in daily mortality (often doubling or tripling overnight).
- **Veterinary Protocol:**
  - Emergency veterinary consultation required. Narrow-spectrum antibiotics (e.g., bacitracin methylene disalicylate, amoxicillin, or lincomycin) administered strictly under prescription.
  - Ensure drinking water acidification (organic acids: citric, propionic, formic acid) to suppress clostridial gut colonization.

## 3. Colibacillosis (*Escherichia coli* Infection)
- **Etiology:** Avian Pathogenic *E. coli* (APEC), leading to colisepticemia, perihepatitis (fibrinous coating on liver), and pericarditis (cloudy heart sac).
- **Key Clinical Symptoms:**
  - Yellowish or off-white watery droppings with pasting around the vent feathers.
  - General flock depression, uneven bird weights, poor FCR, and steady daily mortality.
- **Corrective Actions:**
  - Test and sanitize drinking water (chlorination at 3–5 ppm free available chlorine at the nipple drinker end).
  - Clean and disinfect water storage tanks and flush drinking lines thoroughly.

## 4. Dysbacteriosis (Gut Microflora Imbalance)
- **Key Symptoms:**
  - Undigested whole feed grains visible in wet, greasy, orange/tan droppings.
  - Rapidly caking wet litter causing footpad dermatitis (hock burns and breast blisters).
  - Poor weight gain and rising FCR despite adequate feed consumption.
- **Corrective Actions:**
  - Introduce probiotics, prebiotics, or phytogenic feed additives to restore balanced lactic acid bacteria flora.
  - Verify feed quality for mycotoxin contamination (use broad-spectrum mycotoxin binders in feed).
